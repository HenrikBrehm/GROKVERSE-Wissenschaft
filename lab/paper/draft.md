# DRAFT — Training-set composition steers the Fourier features of grokking transformers, and a 25–50-step history persists under a data switch

*Working draft (session 24, 2026-09-27). Not for circulation. All numbers are copied from the frozen confirmation blocks listed in [[FINDINGS_DETAILS]]; every result section names its registration.
Explorations are marked as such and are not results. Authorship, venue and submission are open decisions of the user ([[paper/STATUS]]).*

## Abstract (draft)

Networks that grok modular arithmetic implement it with a sparse set of Fourier features, and which frequencies a given network uses is usually treated as an arbitrary outcome of initialization.
We show that, with fixed one-hot tokens, an *engineered composition of the training set* — which input pairs are shown — causally steers which frequencies (for the dihedral group D59: which irreducible
representations, measured before grokking) the embedding of a one-layer transformer learns (full-batch AdamW; for addition mod 113 the steering also holds at half and double the learning rate, 0.3× and 3× the weight decay and train fractions 0.2 and 0.5). Pre-registered replications with fresh seeds (10–15 per condition; addition mod 113 unless stated) show the same steering of the embedding in a two-layer transformer at equal step count, a one-layer transformer of 4× width and a shared-embedding MLP, and for non-modular integer addition; in the last case no network generalized within 2000 steps, so there it is a property of the representation only. Even an ordinary uniform random 30 % split biases which frequencies are learned (addition mod 113, pre-registered crossed design, 60 fresh units: networks sharing a split have more similar embedding power spectra after 8000 steps than networks sharing nothing), descriptively more than a shared initialization, but far from determining them (networks with the same split share on average about one of their five strongest frequencies, against about one half without a shared split). In pre-registered, held-out replications with fresh seeds (50 per task), engineered training splits enrich five prescribed
frequencies in addition mod 113 and 97, multiplication mod 113, and composition in D59. In all four tasks (for D59 at 12 000 steps, after grokking in 37/40 runs), equal-norm embedding perturbations along the steered
frequencies reduce the logit margin more than along control frequencies. The choice starts to persist early (addition mod 113; the π window also at add 97 and mul 113, R11): 25 training steps on a first split measurably shape the embedding after 2000 further steps on a pair-disjoint second split, and the sign of an ablation contrast
(old vs new target frequencies removed) reverses between a switch after 25 and after 50 steps (post hoc, the embedding energy points the same way, so this contrast does not separate reliance from energy). Resetting the attention (weights and optimizer state) at a switch after 50 steps reverses the sign of this contrast, whereas the tested
MLP reset barely changes it, and resetting its value path lowers it more than an equal-size reset of its query/key weights — a reset sensitivity, not a storage location. The lock-in also has a time signature (addition mod 113, pre-registered, fresh seeds): a switch after 25 steps delays grokking by a factor of about 1.8, more than a switch after 50 steps, and only if the new split carries different target frequencies — new pairs with the same targets cost no time on average (−0.15 log2; 8/25 seeds slower). Doubling the learning rate during the first 25 steps brings the lock-in forward in the direction the summed learning rate predicts (registered test passed), whereas halving it for 50 steps delays it in the predicted direction but by less than the registered margin (addition mod 113, 20 fresh seeds): the lock-in does not follow the step count alone (descriptively, no seed in either cell leaned toward the step clock by the margin), but summed learning rate was not confirmed as its clock. A follow-up in two other cells (pre-registered, 36 fresh seeds, at a quarter and at double the learning rate, with an embedding-energy measure of persistence) supports that this shortfall comes from Adam's moment windows, which are fixed in steps: rescaling the betas before the switch so that the windows are equal in summed learning rate changed the persistence of the old choice in the predicted direction in both cells (36/36 and 34/36 seeds), and made 100 steps at a quarter of the learning rate equivalent within ±0.1 to 25 default steps (difference +0.015). In addition mod 113 (pre-registered, fresh seeds), engineered splits also generalize earlier: in about 6.5× fewer steps than a uniform random split and 7.5× fewer than a control with exactly the same number of pairs per output (geometric means of times bounded at 8000 steps, 20/20 units each), which shows that the output marginals alone do not produce the speed-up; the arrangement of the pairs within them does. We report effect sizes with confidence intervals, the limits of each measurement (notably that functional sensitivity follows spectral energy), and negative results.

## 1 Introduction (draft prose, session 26; checked against the sources by an independent agent, 13 points corrected)

Small networks trained on modular arithmetic first memorize their training pairs and only much later generalize ("grokking"; Power et al. 2022). Mechanistic analyses showed that the generalizing
solution computes with a handful of Fourier frequencies of the group (Nanda et al. 2023) and, for other finite groups, with a few irreducible representations (Chughtai et al. 2023). *Which*
frequencies a particular network ends up using has been described as arbitrary across runs (Chughtai et al. 2023). Recent theory attributes the competition between frequencies to the initialization
(He et al. 2026a) and, for uniform data over all input sequences, derives the order in which irreducible representations are learned from the Fourier statistics of the input encoding
(Marchetti et al. 2026). With one-hot tokens that encoding favours no frequency in cyclic groups and ties irreducible representations of equal dimension, and the role of the train–test split has
been named as open (He et al. 2026b).

We ask whether the training set itself can decide this choice when its pair structure is engineered to do so (an ordinary random split already biases the choice, far less than an engineered split and without prescribed targets; §3.14). In the one-layer transformer of a previous grokking study we construct training splits whose pair mask carries power at five
prescribed frequencies, and we train with full-batch AdamW and fixed one-hot tokens. The lever differs from the order of examples within an epoch, which LeDoux (2026) showed to set the fundamental
frequency, from the encoding statistics of Marchetti et al. (2026), and from deliberately symmetric training sets, which in feature-learning kernels select the symmetry subgroup the features encode (Tomàs Bernal et al. 2026): here only *which* pairs are shown changes, the split has no built-in symmetry, and the targets are prescribed Fourier frequencies. In pre-registered replications with fresh seeds, the embedding enriches the
prescribed frequencies over disjoint comparison frequencies at step 2000 in every one of 50 seeds per task, for addition mod 113 and mod 97, multiplication mod 113 and composition in the dihedral
group D59 (for D59 this is before grokking; §3.1, §3.6). In all four tasks (D59 after grokking, §3.6), the network is also more sensitive to perturbations along the steered frequencies than along
control frequencies (§3.2, §3.6). Where we examined it (add-113 switch models, exploratory), this sensitivity follows the embedding energy, so we do not claim use beyond energy.

The choice starts to persist early; the persistence and reset results are for addition mod 113 only. After as few as 25 full-batch steps on a first split, a switch to a pair-disjoint second split
leaves a measurable trace in the embedding 2000 steps later (§3.3). Between a switch after 25 and after 50 steps, the sign of an ablation contrast reverses on average: after 25 steps the final model
loses more accuracy when the new target frequencies are removed, after 50 steps when the old ones are removed (§3.4; post hoc, the embedding energy points the same way, so this contrast does not
separate reliance from energy). Early training that later data cannot fully undo is familiar from critical learning periods (Achille et al. 2019), the primacy bias (Nikishin et al. 2022) and warm
starting (Ash & Adams 2020); Lyle et al. (2025) relate grokking to the overwriting of early features and explain recovery by the effective learning rate (their data-switch experiments use CIFAR,
not modular arithmetic), and LeDoux (2026) conjectures a critical period for the frequency choice. Our contribution here is a measured time scale of tens of steps for a specific, prescribed
feature choice against a controlled data switch.

Finally, we ask which part of the network this persistence is sensitive to. Resetting the attention weights together with their optimizer state at the switch after 50 steps reverses the sign of
the ablation contrast (mean 0.86 → −0.63), whereas the tested MLP reset barely changes it (0.79); resetting only the value/output path brings the contrast to about zero, an equal-size reset of the
query/key weights does not (§3.5). Because weights and optimizer state are reset together, the value/output and query/key resets are matched only in parameter count, and the MLP control resets
twice as many parameters, we read this as reset sensitivity, not as the place where the choice is stored. In language models, the ordering within attention is reversed for restoring the ability
to learn a withheld capability (Dong 2026); we discuss this contrast rather than generalize either ordering.

**Contributions.** (i) Causal steering of the learned frequencies (irreps) by an engineered composition of the training set, replicated on held-out seeds across four tasks including a non-abelian group;
(ii) a functional check on three cyclic tasks (margin sensitivity along the steered frequencies), which, where examined, follows the embedding energy and therefore does not show use beyond energy; (iii) an early, time-dependent persistence of the choice under a data switch, with
a reversal of an ablation contrast between 25 and 50 steps; (iv) reset interventions showing that this persistence is more sensitive to resetting attention than to the tested MLP reset, and within
attention to the value/output path than to an equal-size query/key reset. A first-order account of the steering (§5) and all explorations and negative results (§4) are reported separately from
the confirmed results. All claims hold for one model family, one optimizer setting and constructed splits, and the persistence and reset results (§3.3–3.5) for addition mod 113 only (§6).

## 1 Introduction (outline)

- Grokking and Fourier circuits (Power et al. 2022; Nanda et al. 2023); which frequencies/irreps are learned is "arbitrary" per network (Chughtai et al. 2023) and has been linked to initialization
  (He et al. 2026a) and, for uniform data, to the Fourier statistics of the input encoding (Marchetti et al. 2026, AGF).
- Closest work (see [[paper/related_work]], to be checked by a human): LeDoux (2026, arXiv 2603.25047) shows that the *order* of training examples sets the fundamental frequency in modular
  arithmetic; Marchetti et al. (ICML 2026) derive the irrep order from the Fourier statistics of the input *encoding* under uniform data; He et al. (2026a) attribute the competition to initialization.
  Our lever is the complementary one: the *set* of training pairs, full batch, one-hot tokens, predicted in advance by the mask spectrum M0 (we found no AGF-utility bridge for M0, EXP-076).
- Lock-in: critical periods (Achille et al. 2019; Kleinman et al. 2023, 2024), primacy bias (Nikishin et al. 2022), warm starting (Ash & Adams 2020) and, closest, grokking under non-stationarity with the
  effective learning rate as explanation (Lyle et al. 2025). Our contribution is a measured lock-in time of the frequency choice (≈ 25–50 steps) against a pair-disjoint switch and the reversal of an ablation contrast.
  Also to cite (full-text check, session 25, [[paper/fulltext_check_2026-09-27]]): LeDoux (2026) *conjectures* a critical learning period within the first few dozen epochs (fundamental frequency
  identifiable by epoch 3, significant by epoch 14; their §3.4/§6.2) without a switch experiment; Khanh (2026, arXiv 2607.06628) uses the same kind of model (1-layer transformer, full-batch AdamW, embedding power spectrum as circuit identity) and shows that transplanting
  another run's weight *direction* overwrites a run's frequency identity — a weight-space intervention rather than a data switch; Lyle et al. (2025) run warm-start experiments only on
  CIFAR and treat modular arithmetic as stationary (their §4.2), with no frequency analysis. He et al. (2026b, arXiv 2606.02993, §6) name the analysis of the train–test split as open.
- The functional measure (K2-function) is a validation of K2 rather than a separate contribution (frequency ablations go back to Nanda et al. 2023), and we state its limit (function follows energy).
- Partial resets (full-text check, session 25): Dong (2026, arXiv 2607.27281) reports for language models (Pythia up to 1.4B) that after "aging" on base data re-initializing only the
  query–key slices restores learnability (6/6) while the value slices do nothing (0/6). "An attention reset beats an MLP reset" is therefore not new for transformers; our contrast is the
  opposite ordering within attention (value path > query/key) in the grokking/frequency setting, which must be discussed as such (different tasks, scales, measures and reset protocols;
  no claim which is general).
- Contributions: (i) causal steering via split construction, held-out across four tasks incl. a non-abelian group; (ii) a functional validation (margin sensitivity) with its limits; (iii) an early, time-dependent
  lock-in under a data switch; (iv) reset interventions that localize *sensitivity* (not storage) in the attention value path.


## 1b Related work (draft prose, session 25; every entry to be checked by a human against [[paper/related_work]] and [[paper/fulltext_check_2026-09-27]])

**Fourier and irrep circuits.** Networks that grok modular addition compute it with a few Fourier frequencies (Power et al. 2022; Nanda et al. 2023); for finite groups they use irreducible
representations (Chughtai et al. 2023; coset circuits in S₅/S₆: Stander et al. 2024), and which ones a network uses has been described as arbitrary (Chughtai et al. 2023). Recent theory explains the competition between frequencies by
the initialization (He et al. 2026a), analyses irrep selection with the full group table as data and names the role of the train–test split as open (He et al. 2026b, §6), and, within
alternating gradient flow (Kunin et al. 2025), derives the order in which irreps are learned from the Fourier statistics of the input encoding under uniform data (Marchetti et al. 2026;
with one-hot tokens, irreps of equal dimension tie). LeDoux (2026) shows that the *order* of training examples (minibatches) sets the fundamental frequency, while, under a fixed stride ordering,
different random subsets of training pairs gave the same frequency (their §4.2.3, §5.3). Zhou, Zhang and Xu (2024) show outside modular arithmetic that an insufficient, non-uniform sample makes networks learn spurious (aliased) frequencies first; there the sample adds artefacts next to a fixed correct solution, here it selects among equally correct ones. Salah and Yevick (2024, §3.5) restrict the training set of modular addition by symmetry (only a ≥ b; additionally excluding some differences a − b, which mattered only together with a > b) and find that this changes whether and how well an MLP generalizes; which frequencies it learns is not examined. Our lever is the *set* of training pairs under full-batch training with one-hot tokens, with the targets prescribed in advance through the pair mask. This does not contradict LeDoux's random-subset result: there the subsets are random and a strong minibatch ordering dominates, here the subsets are engineered to carry power at chosen frequencies, and full-batch training has no inter-batch ordering (LeDoux, §6.6, calls the ordering mechanism under full batch "not absent" but "degenerate", a single fixed self-interaction). Whether an ordinary *random* split already influences the choice among equally good frequencies we test separately with a crossed design (§3.14): it biases the choice, descriptively more than the initialization, and descriptively far from determining it. On the kernel side, Tomàs Bernal et al. (2026) show for recursive feature machines on modular arithmetic that a deliberately symmetric (H-invariant) training set makes the learned features encode only the invariance subgroup H, and that generalization then requires breaking this symmetry: the structure of the training set selects the group structure the features encode. This is the closest conceptual precedent for our result; it concerns kernels and symmetry subgroups, whereas we select prescribed Fourier frequencies (irreps) in transformers through the spectrum of a non-symmetric pair mask.
More generally, data statistics select among redundant features (Hermann & Lampinen 2020), stage-wise learning follows data spectra in linear networks (Saxe et al. 2014), and neural
networks fit low frequencies first in continuous input spaces (spectral bias / frequency principle; Rahaman et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2019). These do not address which discrete group
frequencies a grokking network adopts.

**Early commitment and lock-in.** Critical learning periods (Achille et al. 2019; Kleinman et al. 2023, 2024), the importance of the early phase of training (Frankle, Schwab and Morcos 2020), the
lasting cost of warm starting (Ash & Adams 2020) and the primacy bias (Nikishin et al. 2022) all describe early training that later data cannot fully undo. Closest to our setting,
Lyle et al. (2025) relate grokking to the overwriting of early features and explain recovery by the effective learning rate; their warm-start experiments use CIFAR and treat modular
arithmetic as stationary, without frequency analysis, so the effective learning rate is an alternative we must address for K4. LeDoux (2026, §3.4) conjectures a critical period in
modular arithmetic without a switch experiment; Howe (2026a) reports that an auxiliary representational (contrastive) prior applied only during the first 2000 epochs (4 % of training) gives most of its benefit (a loss intervention, not a data switch). Khanh (2026) uses the same model class and embedding power spectrum as a circuit identity and shows that transplanting another run's
weight direction overwrites it — a weight-space intervention, whereas we switch the data and measure when the frequency choice becomes persistent.

**Partial resets.** Resetting layers or parameter groups is an established tool to restore plasticity or to probe module criticality (Nikishin et al. 2022; Zhou et al. 2022; Zhang et al.
2022; Chatterji et al. 2020; Asadi et al. 2023 for optimizer resets). For transformers, Dong (2026) reports in language models up to 1.4B parameters that re-initializing only the
query–key slices of attention restores the ability to learn a withheld capability, while value slices do nothing. Our reset results (K4d, K4e) concern a different quantity (persistence
of an early frequency choice) and show the opposite ordering within attention (value path more than query/key); we report this as a contrast, not as a general statement about attention. In our model family, Jia (2026) transfers embeddings, readout and internal blocks between modular tasks and freezes embeddings and readout (query, key and value in one fused matrix), without resets at a data switch and without frequency analysis.

## 2 Setup (draft prose, session 27; to be checked against the code by an independent agent)

**Model and training.** We use the one-layer transformer of the original GROKVERSE study (preset arch25k, following the recipe of Nanda et al. 2023; our implementation has no bias terms): token embedding W_E and learned positional
embedding W_pos (d_model = 128), one causal attention layer with four heads of width 32, a ReLU MLP of width 512, residual connections, no layer normalization and no biases, and an untied
readout W_U. Inputs are the three tokens (a, b, =) with a, b ∈ {0, …, p−1}; the loss is the cross-entropy of the logits at the last position over the p output classes. All weights are
zero-mean Gaussians at initialization, with standard deviation 1/√d_model for W_E, W_pos, W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_in and W_U, 1/√(4·32) for W_O and 1/√512 for W_out. Training is full-batch AdamW (learning rate 10⁻³, weight decay 1.0, β = (0.9, 0.98)) on 30 % of all pairs; K2 runs train for 2000 steps. The two arms
of a seed share the initialization; only the training set differs. Confirmation blocks ran on one GPU (PyTorch 2.9.1 with ROCm, one thread per run) after a statistical (not bitwise) equivalence check against the CPU path on add 113;
all arms and seeds compared within a block ran on the same device.

**Tasks.** Addition modulo 113 (primary) and 97, multiplication modulo 113, and composition in the dihedral group D59 (order 118). For multiplication, pairs with a zero operand are not in
the group; frequencies refer to the discrete-logarithm coordinate (the cyclic group Z_112), and pairs with a zero keep uniform weight in the split construction below. For a cyclic group of
order n, the candidate frequencies are k = 1, …, ⌊(n−1)/2⌋; for D59 they are the 29 two-dimensional irreducible representations, computed from an explicit real basis.

**Engineered splits (K2).** For each seed, a random permutation of the candidate frequencies gives two disjoint target sets T_F and T_G of five frequencies each. Arm F draws its training set
(30 % of all pairs) without replacement with weights q(a, b) = max(0.05, 1 + ε·w(a, b)), where for a cyclic task w(a, b) = (1/5) Σ_{k∈T_F} cos(2πk·u(a)/n + φ_k(c)), u(a) is the group
coordinate of the left operand, c the output and φ_k(c) a random phase per target and output; arm G does the same with T_G. For D59 the cosine is replaced by tr(A_k(c) ρ_k(a)) with random
unit-norm 2×2 matrices A_k(c) and the irrep matrices ρ_k. We use ε = 2. Weighted sampling uses exponential keys (key = U^{1/q}, the largest 30 % are kept). The construction arranges the
target frequencies along the output classes; the token frequencies are no more unequal than in a random split (coefficient of variation 0.14 for the pilot construction), although their spectrum is target-enriched (§5). To the eye an engineered mask is indistinguishable from a random one; the difference lies in its spectrum (Fig. 0: centred mask-spectrum target share 0.194 vs 0.089 for the random arm of the same seed, chance 5/56 ≈ 0.089).

**Switch protocol (K4–K4e).** For the persistence experiments (addition mod 113), a common held-out test set of 20 % of all pairs is removed first; from the remaining pool two training sets
A and B of 3831 pairs each (30 % of all pairs) are drawn with target sets T_A, T_B as above. A and B pick their pairs in alternation from their own weighted key orders, so at overlap ω = 0 (all primary analyses) they share no pair
(a sequential draw would give B the negative image of A's pattern). Alternation keeps B's mask power at T_A at the level of a uniform split (share 0.092 in N28 and P3, chance 5/56 = 0.089; A: 0.18), but it cannot make B neutral there: because A and B are disjoint subsets of one pool, B's first-order W_E gradient at T_A (the first-order mask term of §5 at initialization in the isotropic-Jacobian model, i.e. an expected gradient, not a measured one; left and right slots as separate components, while the shared-path form of §5.2 gives the same means within 0.01 (per seed within 0.06), projection range −0.44 to −0.32) points partly *against* A's, with a signed cosine (mean over the five targets) of −0.53 in N28 and −0.54 in P3 (negative in 30/30 seeds each; `lab/confirm/switch_alignment_s33.py`, split files only, added in session 33). Projected onto A's gradient at T_A (pooled over the targets), B's amounts to about −0.38 of A's own (range −0.42 to −0.33). At first order around initialization, the persistence results are therefore obtained against opposing pressure from the new data, not merely without support; how this term acts after 25–50 steps of training was not measured. The steering strength ε is calibrated on separate calibration seeds so that the mask-spectrum target share of A and B matches
the pilot level (0.185; ε ≈ 2.23 at ω = 0). P3 additionally ran ω = 0.4 (40 % uniformly drawn shared pairs, ε ≈ 5.93) as a descriptive secondary level. A run trains t steps on A, then switches to B (optimizer state kept unless stated) and trains a fixed further budget (2000 steps in P3/N28, 4000
steps in N29–N29c); each seed is run in both directions (A→B, B→A). Reset arms re-initialize the named parameter tensors to their values at initialization and clear their AdamW state at the switch.

**Measures.** All spectral measures use the n group-element rows X of W_E in group order (for mul 113: W_E[g^i], g = 3, i = 0, …, 111). P_k(X) is the power of X in the real two-dimensional Fourier subspace of k (for D59: the four-dimensional span of the matrix coefficients of irrep k),
and P(S) = Σ_{k∈S} P_k for a set S. *Steering* (K2): for a seed with arms F and G (same initialization, disjoint target sets T_F, T_G),
Δ_pow = log[P_F(T_F)/P_F(T_G)] + log[P_G(T_G)/P_G(T_F)] at step 2000, where P_F is measured in arm F's embedding; Δ_pow = 0 means no steering. *Margin sensitivity* (K2-function): with the unit direction
u_S = P_S X / ‖P_S X‖ and X' = X − δ u_S, δ = 0.02 ‖X‖, drop_S is the mean correct-class logit margin on pairs outside both splits before minus after the perturbation; D = drop_own − the mean
drop over four control sets of five frequencies (drawn from a restricted pool that excludes both target sets and the second harmonics 2k of the own targets), averaged over the two arms.
*Persistence index* (K4, K4b): with L = log[P(T_A)/P(T_B)] of the final embedding, π = (L_switch − L_pureB)/(L_pureA − L_pureB), averaged over both switch directions; π is an affine,
unbounded index (0 = the pure second-split endpoint, 1 = the pure first-split endpoint), not a probability. *Ablation contrast* (K4c–e): R = accuracy on the held-out test set after projecting
W_E onto the orthogonal complement of the five *new* target subspaces minus the accuracy after removing the five *old* ones, averaged over both switch directions per seed; R > 0 means that removing
the old frequencies costs more. Each measure is fixed in the frozen registration of its block.

**Protocol.** Each confirmation block was registered before any of its runs: hypotheses, primary measure, minimum effect, family and Holm correction, sample size (from pilot variance), a
reserved block of fresh seeds, and a list of previously seen data and the decisions they influenced. The manifest was committed and names its commit; it stores SHA-256 hashes of the analysis, split and training code;
every run was started through a wrapper that checks the code hashes before, during (every 2 s) and after training and binds each output file to the manifest, and the analysis accepts only bound runs. In P1 (two jobs), N28, N29–N29c, P2d and P2e,
one job of the frozen list was first run as a smoke test and counted as a regular run (from N28 on also through the frozen loader); P2c and P3 had none. Confidence intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals over seeds. Pilots on other seeds are reported
separately as explorations (§4).

### 3.3 Early lock-in under a data switch (K4, K4b; P3 f8adf45, N28 fa154bd)

After 25 steps on a first engineered split and 2000 steps on a pair-disjoint second split, final models sat at 0.351 of the normalized log-energy distance between the two pure endpoints (the persistence index π, P3); the excess over

### 3.14 An ordinary random split also shapes the learned frequencies (R4, manifest 8d89d4c3)

*Drafted in session 30 before any crossed run and before its pilot EXP-014 ran (§4), frozen in session 37 after two Codex rounds, with n = 60 fixed in the registration (not taken from the pilot); fresh seeds 2600–2719; every split drawn uniformly (3831 of 12 769 pairs) from its own stream SeedSequence([tag, u, j]), never from an initialization seed; GPU; sessions 38–39.*
Each unit crosses two initializations with two random splits (4 runs of 8000 steps, add 113, one-layer transformer arch25k, test set = complement). Similarity of two runs is measured on their W_E power spectra (k = 1..56). Per unit, S_split averages the two pairs with the same split and different initializations, S_none the two pairs sharing nothing; Δ_split = S_split − S_none. Primary family (Holm over two, one-sided t-test on Δ − minimum effect, 95 % bootstrap CI over units; confirmed = Holm-p < 0.05 and CI lower bound of Δ − minimum ≥ 0), minimum effects set from the null spread of stored study runs before the block: **H1a** (rank signature before grokking): Spearman at step 2000, Δ_split = **0.33** [0.30, 0.36] (minimum 0.05; 60/60 units above the minimum; Holm-p 4·10⁻²⁶) → **confirmed**. **H1b** (learned choice after grokking; measured at step 8000 whether or not a run had generalized): energy-weighted cosine at step 8000, Δ_split = **0.118** [0.079, 0.157] (minimum 0.04; 48/60 units positive, 42/60 above the minimum; Holm-p 1.7·10⁻⁴) → **confirmed**. *Descriptive (outside the family; the initialization contrast was not a primary test, and for Spearman it partly reflects preserved weak frequencies of the shared initial spectrum):* at every measured step the shared split raised the similarity more than a shared initialization: at step 8000, cosine Δ_init = 0.042 [0.009, 0.076], Δ_split − Δ_init = 0.075 [0.029, 0.121]; Spearman at 2000, Δ_init = 0.125 [0.103, 0.147], Δ_split − Δ_init = 0.205 [0.174, 0.237]. In top-5 terms at step 8000, two networks with the same split and different initializations shared on average 1.05 of their five strongest frequencies (0.49 + 0.56 [0.37, 0.75]), two networks with the same initialization and different splits 0.67, and two sharing nothing 0.49 (chance 25/56 ≈ 0.45). Descriptively it is far from determining the choice: networks trained on the same split share on average about one of their five strongest frequencies, against about one half without a shared split; there is no same-split, same-initialization ceiling in this design. He et al. (2026a) attribute the choice to the initialization (spectral magnitude and phase); R4 does not refute this, since a shared initialization also raised similarity (Δ_init, descriptive), but it shows that the split contributes as well, descriptively more.

### 3.15 Rescaling Adam's moment windows to summed learning rate removes most of the step dependence of lock-in (equivalent at lr × 0.25, direction confirmed at lr × 2; R9, manifest 8b22879a)

*Registered after R3 (§3.10) and the pilots EXP-081, EXP-089, EXP-090 and EXP-091 (§4; named in the registration with the decisions they informed: Λ = 25 with t = 100, the β rescaling rule, the equivalence margin ±0.1, c = 2 with t = 15, and testing H3 against 0), design and code checked by an independent read-only agent before freezing (after which H1 was changed from a test against the minimum effect 0.05 to a test against 0 and n raised from 24 to 36 by a power simulation with Holm and the CI rule); frozen before any run; 36 fresh seeds (3300–3335); GPU; session 40.*
Protocol as in §4 (EXP-089/090): add 113, one-layer transformer, pair-disjoint ω = 0 split pairs built from each seed, a switch A → B and B → A after t steps, then 2000 steps at the default learning rate and betas; π = persistence of the old choice in the log energy ratio of W_E group power, from the final embedding against pure A and B runs of the same seed, averaged over both directions. Before the switch the learning rate is scaled by c, so that Λ = c·t in default steps; in the rescaled arms the betas are also changed before the switch to β′ = 1 − c(1 − β), which makes Adam's moment windows equal in Λ (1 − β′ = c(1 − β); β′ = 0.975/0.995 for c = 0.25 and 0.8/0.96 for c = 2). **H1 (direction at c = 0.25, Λ = 25):** e₁ = π(default β) − π(β′) = **+0.161** [0.132, 0.192] (36/36 seeds > 0; Holm-p 3·10⁻¹²; confirmed = Holm-p < 0.05 and CI lower bound > 0; the lower bound is also above the originally planned minimum effect 0.05, descriptive). **H2 (equivalence at Λ = 25):** d₁ = π(c = 0.25, t = 100, β′) − π(default, t = 25) = **+0.015** [0.005, 0.027] (TOST against ±0.1, Holm-p 3·10⁻¹⁶). **H3 (second level, c = 2, Λ = 30):** e₂ = π(β′) − π(default β) = **+0.057** [0.042, 0.073] (34/36; Holm-p 1.5·10⁻⁸; per-seed values in Figure 9, `lab/paper/figures/fig9_r9_window_clock.py`). All three confirmed; registered reading **"window rule confirmed"**. 36/36 seeds valid (none excluded for too small a gap between the pure runs); one run failed technically in the first pass (a Windows file-access error when renaming its result file) and was recomputed and bound in a second pass of the same job list.
*Descriptive (outside the family):* d₂ = π(c = 2, t = 15, β′) − π(default, t = 30) = −0.007 [−0.021, 0.009]. Arm means of π (post hoc): default t = 25 0.34, t = 30 0.44; c = 0.25 0.51 with default and 0.35 with rescaled betas; c = 2 0.38 with default and 0.44 with rescaled betas. The separation of the two frequency sets in W_E at the switch (0.034 / 0.052 / 0.035 for t = 25 / c = 0.25 default / c = 0.25 rescaled; 0.047 / 0.039 / 0.046 for t = 30 / c = 2 default / c = 2 rescaled), which does not depend on Adam's step counter, points the same way; the rescaled c = 0.25 arm keeps 0.06 of the gap between the unrescaled arm and t = 25.
So, consistent with the deviation from a pure Λ clock seen in §3.10 (whose cells were not re-tested), the deviation runs in both directions as a moment lag counted in steps predicts (R9 rescaled both betas; the pilots EXP-090 and EXP-081 point to the first moment): with fewer, larger steps (c = 2) the default windows are longer in Λ and lock-in is weaker than Λ predicts; with more, smaller steps (c = 0.25) they are shorter and lock-in is stronger; rescaling the betas so that the windows are equal in Λ reduces the difference to about a tenth in both cells (at c = 0.25 from 0.18 to 0.015, equivalent within ±0.1; at c = 2 from −0.06 to −0.007, descriptive). The rescaling 1 − β′ = c(1 − β) is the deterministic continuous-time matching in which 1 − β shrinks in proportion to the step size (the regime of the continuous-time analysis of Adam by Barakat & Bianchi, arXiv 1810.02263, Assumption 2.5: (1 − β(γ))/γ tends to a constant as the step size γ → 0); it differs from Malladi et al.'s SDE square-root rule (2022, Definition 5.2: η′ = √κ·η and 1 − β′ = κ(1 − β), i.e. 1 − β′ = c²(1 − β) in terms of the learning-rate factor c), which matches gradient noise across batch sizes. That Adam's first moment is a memory on a fixed step clock, which makes data-order effects first order in the learning rate, has been shown for fine-tuning (Sweeney 2026, arXiv 2606.29554). R9 is therefore an application of known optimization principles; what is tested here is their consequence for the persistence of the early frequency choice.
**Limits:** one task (add 113), one architecture, full batch, two (c, Λ) pairs; π is a representation measure (W_E energy), no ablation; H2 means "equivalent within ±0.1 although Adam's step counter differs after the switch (100 against 25)", and the small positive rest (+0.015, CI excluding 0) is compatible with that difference; the R3 cells and its accuracy measure were not re-tested; why a shorter lag makes the early motion more frequency-selective is open.

*Registered after the pilots EXP-107/108 (lr 10⁻³, seeds 3700–3707) and EXP-109 (lr 2·10⁻³, seeds 3700–3701), which the registration lists with every decision they informed (band, grid, second learning rate, H2 reformulated after EXP-109 from "α(2·10⁻³) in the band" to the paired difference; full-batch arm dropped before the EXP-109 result for cost); design and code checked twice by independent read-only agents; frozen before any run; 10 fresh seeds (3900–3909, targets from SeedSequence([100300, seed, 0])); GPU; session 47; 3 infrastructure failures (Windows file lock) re-run with the identical job line before the analysis; recomputed by an independent agent with its own code (all values equal to ≤ 4.4·10⁻¹⁶).*
Protocol: add 113, 30 % data (N = 3831), the L0 construction; AdamW (weight decay 1, β = 0.9/0.98) with i.i.d. minibatches of 1024, 256 and 64 at lr 10⁻³ and 2·10⁻³; per cell pure runs F and G and switches F → G and G → F at six steps; every run 5000 steps; π(t) from the W_E group power at step 5000 (mean over both directions); t½ = first upward crossing of 0.5 (linear interpolation from (0, 0)); α = least-squares slope of log t½ against log(N/B) per seed. H1: α(10⁻³) in [0.3, 0.7] (TOST); H2: paired Δα = α(2·10⁻³) − α(10⁻³) > 0; Holm over both. All seeds valid, no censoring.
**H1:** α = **0.583** [0.560, 0.606], Holm-p 5·10⁻⁶; t½ = 58, 114, 289 steps at batch 1024, 256, 64 (EXP-108 on other seeds: 62, 127, 290). **H2:** Δα = **+0.139** [0.098, 0.178], 10/10, Holm-p 6·10⁻⁵. Secondary: α(2·10⁻³) = 0.722 [0.673, 0.767]; t½(2·10⁻³)/t½(10⁻³) = 0.58 [0.54, 0.62], 0.64 [0.59, 0.68], 0.85 [0.78, 0.92] at batch 1024, 256, 64.
So under i.i.d. minibatches, the number of steps to lock-in grows on average about as the square root of the steps per epoch at the default learning rate, and doubling the learning rate shortens it almost by half at large batches but much less at small ones. The simplest sign-drift model (t½ ∝ 1/(lr·√B), derived in this project from the sign model of Li et al., 2024) does not suffice. **Limits:** log t½ is curved (two-point slopes at lr 10⁻³ 0.48 and 0.68; Δα between 1024 and 256 only +0.07 [−0.01, 0.14], between 256 and 64 +0.21 [0.13, 0.29]), so α is an average slope over N/B = 3.7–60, not a clean power law; energy measure π, no ablation; addition mod 113, one architecture, weight decay 1, two learning rates; π read at step 5000. A model comparison fixed before the results (exploration, six cell means) favoured t½ ≈ 23.6·√(N/B)/λ + 2.2·(N/B) (λ = lr/10⁻³) over a free power law (RMS of log residuals 0.088 against 0.121); its predictions for lr 4·10⁻³ are recorded (JOURNAL, session 47) and untested.

- Lock-in is smooth in the number of pre-switch steps (π ≈ 0.01, 0.19, 0.30, 0.56, 1.05 at t = 10, 20, 25, 35, 50; pilot, 10 known seeds); neither the W_E lead nor OV alignment at the switch predicts
  it better than the step count (EXP-060, EXP-070, EXP-071).
- *Random splits (association; crossed pilot).* For ordinary random 30 % splits of add 113 (baseline runs, seeds 10–19), the mask spectrum M0 of a run's own split ranks its final top-5 W_E frequencies better than chance and better than other runs' masks (pre-registered test decided at n = 8, seeds 10–17: mean normalized rank 0.277 vs 0.499 with foreign masks; 0.279 vs 0.492 with all 10 seeds, reported afterwards; interpreted as an association only after a later review, EXP-015). Split and initialization of these runs were drawn from identically seeded generators, so this does not separate the split from the seed; a crossed init × split pilot (EXP-014, seeds 10–19, 5 units, CPU; session 39) found no significant difference in top-5 overlap between split and initialization (registered reading "no dominant factor", p = 0.41), while the final frequencies ranked high under the mask spectrum of the *used* split and at chance under the unused split of the same initialization (mean normalized rank 0.21 vs 0.55, 5/5 pairs, p = 1/32). The registered confirmation R4 (§3.14) shows that a random split biases the choice (descriptively more than the initialization).
- The mask spectrum M0 predicts the learned frequencies (EXP-015) and is closely related, at first order, to the power of the first W_E gradient (coherent variant M0c for the transformer;
EXP-050: first order explains only part; [[BEFUND-split-mechanismus]]); it is *not* the first-stage utility of
  the simplest AGF model (EXP-076, negative).

  ≈ 0 on the continuous measure (0.02 ± 0.65, post hoc).

- *Engineered splits grok much earlier (observation, post hoc, confounded).* On the same device the engineered splits reached test accuracy ≥ 0.95 (the trainer's generalization event) after a median of 662 steps (pilot EXP-016, CPU, 20 runs) versus 5975 for random splits (10 runs); in EXP-047 (same seeds, initialization, size and token frequencies) 20/20 engineered arms generalized within 2000 steps (median 688) and 0/20 arms with shuffled pair structure did. These test sets were complements of the training split. With a common held-out test set (EXP-041 runs on the EXP-018 splits, seeds 20–29, CPU, 2150 steps; not analysed for speed at the time) the engineered arm generalized in 9/10 seeds (425–1775 steps) and the uniform random arm in 0/10 (test accuracy ≤ 0.15), so test-set composition does not explain the gap. Whether spectral concentration rather than any structured non-uniformity is responsible was tested by the pre-registered pilot EXP-018 with a control whose sampling weights are permuted within each antidiagonal (matched in weights, not exactly in the realized per-output counts, which differ by ≈ 3 of ≈ 34 pairs; result below; its engineered and random arms were already seen through EXP-041). This would run against the frequency-principle expectation that a training spectrum mismatched to the test set delays generalization (Zhou, Zhang and Xu 2024). That the choice of training data changes grokking speed is known in another form: frequency-aware sampling that balances token updates accelerates grokking (AlQuabeh et al. 2025, arXiv 2505.15624, abstract checked). Here token frequencies do not explain the gap: in EXP-047 the shuffled arms had exactly the token frequencies of the engineered arms and did not generalize. Literature search: [[paper/lit_grokking_speed_2026-09-27]].
  - *Pre-registered pilot EXP-018 (session 32; control arm prospective):* on a common held-out test set, engineered splits E generalized 2.69 log2 (≈ 6.5×) earlier on average than a control D with the same weight distribution within every anti-diagonal (weights permuted within anti-diagonals; 10/10 seeds, one-sided p = 0.001; time bounded at 8000 steps). D was about as slow as a uniform random split (log2 ratio 0.17, n.s.). In this pilot the speed-up required the engineered pair arrangement that carries the spectral concentration, not only the output marginals. The E and R arms of these seeds had been seen before (EXP-041); the confirmation R5 (n = 20 by its pre-registered power rule) confirmed both contrasts on fresh units (§3.9).
  - *Dose curve with a prediction fixed before the runs (EXP-084, session 32, 10 fresh units, pilot):* We calibrated two phenomenological models on seen runs only. In M_share, time depends on the realized mask target share s through b = T∞ + A/(s − s0). In M_eps, log time is linear in log ε. The two models differed at extreme ε, where s saturates (≈ 0.40–0.43): M_share predicted saturation, M_eps a continued speed-up. The observed median log2(b(ε 32)/b(ε 8)) was −0.11 (M_share −0.14, M_eps −0.82), and ε = 4 fell inside the pre-set band [450, 575], at its lower edge (median 450 steps; M_share predicted 514, M_eps 582). So above ε = 2 the speed-up in this pilot is consistent with following the pair spectrum rather than the extremity of the draw weights. At the low end the model failed: ε = 1 was far slower than predicted (1/10 units within 4000 steps, prediction ≈ 1900) (`lab/experiments/EXP-084-dose-curve/`).
  - *Planting the early signal (EXP-087 pilot and EXP-088, session 39; fresh seeds, rules fixed before any result was read (some additions after the runs had started), no frozen manifest; exploration).* To ask whether the R5 speed-up runs through the early W_E target signal, we added a fixed component on the five target frequencies to the number rows of W_E right after initialization of a uniform random split ("planting": power C × the median initial frequency power per target, random directions from a separate stream; control: an unstructured addition of equal Frobenius norm). In the pilot (10 units, C = 2) planting shortened the bounded time of the random split by 3.42 log2 [3.08, 3.75] (10/10; a lower bound, 3 random-split runs censored at 8000 steps), and the norm control had no effect (−0.09 [−0.33, 0.12]). The planted random split was even faster than the engineered split E2 (−0.63 [−0.99, −0.20]) because the calibration overshot (target share of W_E energy at step 250: 0.40 vs 0.31), so the pre-set mediation reading was "unclear"; across the five arms the share at step 250 ordered the times exactly. EXP-088 (20 fresh units; data arms ε = 2, 4, 8; planting arms C = 1, 2, 4; R descriptive) then placed planted arms on the data dose curve: log2 time linear in the share at step 250, fitted over the three data arms with unit intercepts and a common slope, planted arms counted only inside the share range of their unit's data arms (± 0.03, no further extrapolation). The mean residual of the planted arms was −0.39 log2 (90 % CI [−0.52, −0.27], 95 % CI [−0.55, −0.24], 17/20 units negative), i.e. they generalized ≈ ×1.3 faster than their share predicts; the pre-set equivalence bound ±0.5 log2 (90 % CI) was narrowly missed (reading "unclear"; the same under the registered piecewise-linear sensitivity curve). The direction (planting slightly faster) was known from EXP-087 before EXP-088 was registered. Relative to the full data effect R → E8 (≥ 3.75 log2; R censored at 8000 steps in 3/20 units) the residual is ≈ 10 % or less. *Interpretation (not a claim):* the early target share is consistent with being the main mediator of the speed-up; in this measure the split adds nothing beyond it, and the direct intervention is slightly more effective per unit of share (possibly because it acts from step 0). Planting also steered the final top-5 almost like the data (3.45–3.65 vs 3.65–4.00 of 5 targets, EXP-088). That well-aligned early embeddings speed up grokking is known (Howe 2026b, arXiv 2607.04333; GrokTransfer, arXiv 2504.13292); at most the near-equivalence of a data-driven and a planted early signal on a common dose axis was not found in our search. **Limits:** add 113, one architecture; only C = 2 lies inside the data range (± 0.03) in every unit (C = 1 in 4, C = 4 in 7 units), and the three planting arms of a unit share directions; E4 and E8 are close in share (0.45 / 0.49); the share at 250 is measured after the intervention and so contains the planted part by design (`lab/experiments/EXP-087-planted-signal-mediation/`, `lab/experiments/EXP-088-common-dose-curve/`).

- *Early data switches slowed generalization in stored runs; later ones did not (observation, post hoc, session 32; **confirmed on 30 fresh seeds by R6, §3.8**).* The stored switch blocks reused seeds whose frequencies we had already analysed; only their generalization steps were new. The question itself arose in the same session from EXP-020/EXP-041. We bounded the time to the trainer's generalization event at a common 2000 steps from step 0. We compared a switch with training on the destination split alone, using the log2 time ratio (averaged over both directions where both exist). For add 113 the pattern was:
  - A switch after 25 steps generalized later in 28/30 seeds of N28 (mean 0.54 log2, ≈ ×1.45) and in 28/30 seeds of P3 (sign stated before reading the P3 times; mean 0.60 under the common cap).
  - In EXP-056 the median was positive at every overlap level.
  - A switch after 50 steps generalized *earlier* than the destination-only run in 24/30 N28 seeds. The difference between 25 and 50 had mean 0.90 log2 (27/30).

It stayed positive in 8/10 seeds in each case (`speed_switch_optstate_s32.py`). So between about a quarter and just over half of the delay disappeared with the optimizer state. No optimizer reset removed the rest. That the rest sits in the weights is an inference, not a measurement.
  - A possible reason why a switch after the lock-in speeds generalization up: when the new split reaches 99 % training accuracy, test accuracy is already 0.42 (median over runs) after a switch at 50, against 0.16 in the destination-only run at the same step (30/30 seeds). At 25 the pre-stated comparison (destination-only at its own memorization step) was also positive (25/30), but under the same-step control, added after the first look, the difference vanished (15/30) (`speed_switch_fitroute_s32.py`).

The overlap result (more shared pairs, more delay) fits that reading at least as well. A control that switches between two splits with the same target frequencies (EXP-086) decides between the two. *Pilot (session 37; seeds 400–409, known from EXP-056; rule and prediction fixed before the runs):* a switch at t = 25 to a pair-disjoint split A2 with the **same** five targets and matched steering strength did not cost time (C = −0.26 log2, 4/10 seeds positive), whereas the switch to other targets did (+0.57, 8/10); the difference D = +0.82 log2 (9/10 seeds, exact one-sided sign test p = 0.011). In first order, A2 pushes against the target directions of A as strongly as B does (gradient cosine −0.52 vs −0.53), so this first-order counter-term does not explain the cost. This favoured a conflict between target sets over a generic early-change cost; the confirmation R8 on 25 fresh seeds holds (D = +0.81 log2 [0.57, 1.04], 24/25; §3.8).

The comparison replaces the first t destination updates by origin updates, so it mixes an interruption of the lock-in with less destination exposure and more distinct pairs. Less exposure alone would predict a larger delay at 50 than at 25, the opposite of N28 (`lab/confirm/speed_switch_cost_*_s32.py`, [[confirm/R6_REGISTRATION_DRAFT|R6]]).
- *What sets the lock-in clock: Adam's first-moment window (EXP-081, EXP-089, EXP-090, EXP-091; session 40; fresh seeds except EXP-091, rules and code fixed before the runs, the trainer changes and the EXP-089 result checked by an independent read-only agent, no frozen manifest; exploration; the main contrasts of EXP-090 and EXP-091 were later confirmed on fresh seeds in R9, §3.15).* All three use the N28/EXP-059 protocol (add 113, pair-disjoint ω = 0 split pairs, switch A → B and B → A at t, then 2000 steps (EXP-089/090/091: default learning rate and betas after the switch; EXP-081: the cell's betas throughout); π = persistence of the old choice in the log energy ratio, against pure runs of the same seed and, in EXP-081, of the same β cell). **Fixed Λ, more steps (EXP-089, 10 seeds):** with the learning rate scaled by 0.25 for 100 steps (Λ = 25, as 25 default steps), π was higher than after 25 default steps, +0.24 [0.17, 0.34] (95 % bootstrap CI; 10/10 seeds), about as high as after 33 default steps. The difference was already present in W_E at the switch. So the §3.10 shortfall from a pure Λ clock reappears on fresh seeds with a different measure. **Equal windows in Λ (EXP-090, 16 seeds):** Adam averages over a fixed number of *steps* (≈ 10 for β₁ = 0.9), so at lr × 0.25 its windows are four times shorter in Λ. Rescaling the betas only before the switch so that the windows are equal in Λ (1 − β′ = c(1 − β), i.e. β′ = 0.975/0.995; bias-corrected moments kept continuous at the switch) removed the difference: π(lr × 0.25, 100 steps, β′) − π(25 default steps) = +0.002 [−0.024, 0.024], while the replication without rescaling gave +0.16 [0.09, 0.24] and the paired contrast between the two 100-step arms was +0.16 [0.10, 0.23] (15/16). Rescaling β₁ alone was enough (−0.02 relative to 25 default steps, slightly below). A caveat for the comparisons with the 25-step arm (not for the paired contrast between the two 100-step arms): after the switch Adam's step counter stands at 100 instead of 25; the separation of the two frequency sets in W_E at the switch, which does not depend on it, gives the same reading (ratio 0.04 of the unrescaled gap). **Larger learning rate (EXP-091, the EXP-089 seeds, 10 seeds, pilot):** at lr × 2 for 15 steps (Λ = 30) the default windows are twice as long in Λ; rescaling the betas to 0.8/0.96 raised π by +0.05 [0.02, 0.08] (10/10), from about 27 to about 29 equivalent default steps (Λ = 30 would give 30; from cell means, coarse). **Varying β₁ at the standard learning rate (EXP-081, 10 seeds):** zeroing Adam's first moment at t = 25 lowered π by 0.08 / 0.14 / 0.19 for β₁ = 0.8 / 0.9 / 0.95 (contrast 0.95 vs 0.8 positive in 9/10 seeds, sign test p = 0.011), of the same order and trend as the momentum tail of a scalar Adam model (estimated 2.7 / 8.0 / 17.4 against predicted 4.0–4.5 / 8.7–10.8 / 16–24 update equivalents; two of three below the predicted range). The lock-in window did not scale with the β₂ horizon (β₂ = 0.95 or 0.995 changed π at t = 33 by −0.05 and +0.01; a 1/(1 − β₂) scaling would have predicted about +0.5 and −0.5). Post hoc, the lock-in present at the switch without the tail fell with β₁ (π after zeroing 0.34 / 0.21 / 0.13; ordered in 9/10 seeds). *Interpretation (not a claim):* in these pilots (one Λ per learning-rate factor, c = 0.25 and 2) the early lock-in follows summed learning rate once Adam's first-moment lag is also measured in summed learning rate; a longer lag makes the early motion less frequency-selective, and the lag's tail after a switch adds to persistence. The β rescaling (1 − β ∝ learning rate) is the deterministic continuous-time matching (cf. ## 5 Theory (first order, outline)

Under near-uniform initial outputs, the first gradient on embedding row t is a sum over the training pairs containing t, mapped through the initial Jacobian; under isotropic Jacobians its expected Fourier power at k is
proportional to the coherent mask spectrum M0c minus a marginal term for the transformer (shared path for both positions) and to M0 minus marginal terms for the MLP (independent halves). The output-uniform part of a row's own target vector cancels exactly (the row's pair count still scales its gradient); the other operand's token frequencies do not (corrected in session 25, see 5.2), so the prediction for a frequency-matched
control with shuffled pairs (no steering) rests on the near-uniform marginals of the engineered splits, whose spectrum is nevertheless target-enriched (5.2); this is consistent with pilot EXP-047, not a confirmation. Derivation and its limits: [[BEFUND-split-mechanismus]].

**5.1 Setting (draft text, session 25; post hoc explanation, not an independent test).** Let m(a, b) ∈ {0, 1} mark the training pairs, N = Σ m, and c = a + b mod p. At
initialization the logits are small, so the softmax output is close to uniform, s ≈ 𝟙/p, and the cross-entropy gradient with respect to the logits of pair (a, b) is
s − e_c ≈ −P⊥ e_c with P⊥ = I − 𝟙𝟙ᵀ/p. Write J_L, J_R for the Jacobians of the logits with respect to the embedding of the left and right operand, assumed at first order to be
the same for all pairs. The gradient on embedding row t is then

  g_t ≈ −(1/N) [ J_Lᵀ P⊥ ℓ_t + J_Rᵀ P⊥ r_t ],  ℓ_t(c) = m(t, c − t),  r_t(c) = m(c − t, t).

**5.2 Frequency content.** Taking the DFT over the token index t (ℓ̂_k(c) = Σ_t ℓ_t(c) ω^{−kt}, ω = e^{2πi/p}) and assuming isotropic random Jacobians at initialization,

- *shared path* (transformer: at initialization the nearly uniform attention mixes both positions into the same value/output path, J_L ≈ J_R; neglecting the attention-score derivative and the pair dependence of the MLP gating at initialization):
  E‖ĝ_k‖² ∝ ‖P⊥(ℓ̂_k + r̂_k)‖² = Σ_c |ℓ̂_k(c) + r̂_k(c)|² − |n̂_L(k) + n̂_R(k)|²/p, the *coherent mask spectrum* M0c minus a marginal term;
- *separate paths* (shared-embedding MLP: a and b enter different halves of W_in, J_L and J_R independent): the cross term averages out,
  E‖ĝ_k‖² ∝ ‖P⊥ℓ̂_k‖² + ‖P⊥r̂_k‖², the *mask spectrum* M0 minus marginal terms.

Here n̂_L(k) = Σ_c ℓ̂_k(c) is the DFT of how often each token occurs as a left operand (likewise n̂_R). The subtracted term is the power (p·|mean|²) of the c-mean component of ℓ̂_k(c) + r̂_k(c): P⊥ removes
every component that does not vary along the output c. What cancels exactly is therefore the output-uniform part of a row's *own* target vector (its n_t u term); the row's pair count still scales its gradient (‖P⊥ℓ_t‖² = n_t(1 − n_t/p) for 0/1 entries). Token frequencies still enter through the
*other* operand: for m(a, b) = ρ(a), ℓ̂_k(c) = ρ̂(k) is constant in c and vanishes, but r̂_k(c) = ω^{−kc} ρ̂(k)* is not, and the shared-path power is p |ρ̂(k)|² (checked numerically,
session 25). **Prediction 1 (quantitative, not "marginals never steer"):** a control that keeps the a/b token frequencies of an engineered split but shuffles its pairs keeps only the
marginal contribution (plus random pair noise); for the EXP-016 construction the marginals are no more unequal than in a random split (coefficient of variation 0.14), although their spectrum is target-enriched
(mean target rank 11.6 vs 27.5 by chance); after centring, the first-order spectrum of the shuffled splits is at chance (shared path, recomputed in session 25: mean 0-based target rank
26.2 vs 27.5, share 0.090 vs 0.191 for the engineered splits), so such a control is predicted to steer at most weakly. A split with strongly structured token frequencies would be predicted to steer towards the frequencies of ρ.
**Prediction 2 (architecture contrast):** the first gradient of the transformer should follow M0c, that of the shared-embedding MLP M0.

**5.3 Status of the predictions (all exploratory, known seeds).** Prediction 2 matches the measured first gradients (Spearman ρ 0.77 for M0c vs 0.57 for M0 in the transformer; 0.24 vs 0.37 in
the MLP, 9/10 seeds), measured before the derivation was written, so it is an explanation rather than a test. The expected outcome for pilot EXP-047 (no steering by the shuffled control) was noted before its runs, but derived
from the uncorrected argument that all token frequencies cancel; the corrected quantitative form (§5.2) was written after the result. A control with exactly matched a/b token frequencies
but random pair structure showed no detectable steering (Δ̄ 0.3 on the top-5 overlap score, p = 0.33), while the pair structure did (D̄ 6.8, 10/10 seeds, p = 0.001); a pilot on 10 seeds,
not a confirmation. For the *final* frequencies of the transformer, the registered check EXP-019 (rule fixed before the runs; 12 units: EXP-018 random-split arm, GPU, plus two CPU baselines) found M0c better than M0 (r̄ 0.232 vs 0.315, mean paired difference 0.082, one-sided sign-flip p = 0.0012; secondary random-split arm D, n = 10: p = 0.004); adding the initial W_E energy improved the prediction further (descriptive, p = 0.022). For the *final* frequencies of the MLP, M0 is only descriptively better than M0c (r̄ 0.323 vs 0.351, lower is better; 6/10 seeds, p ≈ 0.21). The order *within* the target set, by contrast, does not follow the prediction (session 28, pilot EXP-045, 10 seeds, `lab/confirm/EXP045_posthoc_within.py`): at step 2000 the MLP's target order follows M0c (mean within-arm ρ 0.60) more than M0 (0.46; the predicted M0 > M0c holds in 3/10 seeds, one-sided p 0.92), and by step 10 000 both have faded (0.17 / 0.20). Prediction 2 therefore holds for the first gradient but not for the later order within the target set; M0c predicting the fine order (C1) is not specific evidence for the shared-path derivation.

**5.4 Limits of the first-order argument.** (i) It describes the gradient at initialization. With AdamW the first update is approximately lr · sign(g) coordinate-wise (bias-corrected
moments give m̂/√v̂ = g/|g| at step 1). This sign-like update, to which the decoupled weight-decay term −lr·λ·W_E(0) adds the initialization spectrum, keeps the dominant frequencies but can spread some power to other frequencies; the spectrum of the *update* is therefore not exactly the one derived above. In a simulation of the first-order model (isotropic random Jacobian, d = 128, P1 construction on 10 calibration
seeds × 2 arms, session 25, numpy only) the coordinate-wise sign of the gradient kept all five targets in its top five in every case (target share of the power 0.152 vs 0.191 for
the gradient itself); this checks only the model, not the network. In a
two-hot quadratic pilot network with an activation switch (EXP-033, exploratory), replacing AdamW by plain gradient descent in the first phase only (to matched amplitude; the second phase
was always AdamW; one stage tested) still gave a persistent bias towards the split's targets, slightly stronger (ratio 1.26 [1.10, 1.46]); sign-GD reproduced AdamW. Whether this holds for
the transformer is untested. (ii) It describes the initial gradient (and, approximately, the first sign-like Adam updates), i.e. which frequencies the network starts to grow, not which survive: in the engineered splits some early-weak
targets were displaced by step 2000 by second harmonics fold(2k) of targets of the same arm (EXP-016, post hoc, 14/14 in seeds 10–15; the registered check EXP-021, whose rule was fixed before its 18 runs existed but whose data were partly seen descriptively, found 22 of 23 non-target top-5 members to be fold(2k) of the arm's own targets, expected 2.2, Poisson-binomial p = 7e-22, and 0/23 for the other arm's targets; session 36). In the MLP pilot EXP-045 the W_E top-5 contained no non-target member in any of 20 runs, so the question whether even harmonics are transformer-specific (He et al. 2026a report only odd harmonics in the input weights of a two-layer ReLU network) cannot be answered from the top-5 (N45, exploration). (iii) The isotropy assumption on the Jacobians is not checked beyond the first-gradient
correlations. (iv) It says nothing about why the early choice persists after a data switch (K4); the lab's amplitude model for that (H40) was tested by EXP-040 and not supported (pre-registered pilot, 4 known seeds; registered reading "unclear", no seed shifted in the predicted direction); the lock-in clock is treated empirically in §4, §3.10, §3.15, §3.16 and §3.17 (EXP-081/089–095, R3, R9, R10, R11); the first-order account makes no prediction about it.
The derivation in the simplest alternating-gradient-flow model (AGF) does *not* reduce to M0 (EXP-076, negative), so the link to AGF-type theories remains open.

## 5b Discussion (draft, session 25)

**What the data choose.** Which Fourier features a grokking network uses is not only a matter of initialization: a training set with a prescribed pair structure moves the choice
towards prescribed frequencies across three cyclic tasks and a non-abelian group. A first-order argument (§5, post hoc) accounts for part of this through how the training pairs enter the first gradient on the embedding;
the derivation also says what should *not* matter (a row's own occurrence count) and what should (the arrangement of pairs along the output classes, including the token frequencies of
the other operand). The last point is a prediction we have not yet tested (pilot EXP-077 prepared); it would test whether marginals alone can steer (against the reading that only
non-marginal pair structure steers), without by itself isolating the first-order route. *Added session 28 (exploration, §4):* the realized pair spectrum also goes with the *order* within the prescribed set, in every task and architecture we looked at, and in the transformer this order outlasts generalization; but the MLP shows the same M0c > M0 preference, so the architecture-specific form of the derivation does not carry it. Whether a prescribed order is causally imposed is prepared as an intervention (EXP-082, graded target amplitudes); three correlational predictions are registered on blocks that have not yet run (C1–C3).

**How early and where.** The choice becomes persistent within tens of optimizer steps: after a switch at 50 steps, removing the old frequencies from the final network costs more than removing
the new ones, after a switch at 25 steps the reverse (an ablation contrast that does not separate reliance from energy). Resetting the attention, and within it the value/output path, at
the switch reverses much of this contrast; the tested MLP reset does little.
Two cautions frame this. First, the resets show where the persistence is *sensitive*, not where it is stored: weights and optimizer state were reset together, and in the same model class
transplanting MLP weight directions alone appears to move frequency identity (Khanh 2026, suggestive, three pairs per task). Second, in language models the ordering within attention is reversed for restoring the ability to learn a new capability
(query–key rather than value; Dong 2026). One reading, untested, is that the relevant path is the one the new data require to change: our switch keeps the task and the routing and changes
only the content.

**Open questions.** (i) Which state variable makes the early choice persistent? No measured quantity we tried (W_E lead, OV alignment) explains the time course better than optimizer time (steps or summed learning rate; R3 shows the step count itself is not the clock, §3.10)
(EXP-060, 070, 071); an amplitude-race account was tested and not supported (EXP-040, pre-registered pilot, 4 known seeds: rescaling the non-DC part of the initial number-token embeddings (around their mean) by 0.5 or 2 left the lock-in window at 25–50 steps in 3/4 seeds, and no seed shifted in the predicted direction; registered reading "unclear"). (ii) Do the persistence results hold beyond add 113 and this architecture (pilots: EXP-074 on add 97/mul 113 "unclear", same direction as add 113 but weaker; EXP-075 in an MLP not analysable, 5/10 valid seeds)? *(Session 46: for the π window at add 97 and mul 113, yes, R11 §3.17; open for the functional and time measures and other architectures.)* (iii) Is the
steering robust to learning rate, weight decay and training fraction (registration R1)? (iv) Can the first-order account be connected to theories of the learning order under uniform data
(AGF; the simplest bridge failed, EXP-076)? (v) An ordinary random split biases the choice (R4, §3.14; descriptively more than the initialization and far from determining it); which property of the split carries this bias is untested.

## 6 Limitations

One architecture family and optimizer for all persistence claims (the representation-level lock-in window was confirmed at add 113, add 97 and mul 113, §3.17; the functional contrast K4c, the time cost (R6/R8), K4b and the reset results only at add 113; the pilots EXP-074, EXP-075 on other tasks and an MLP were not conclusive); engineered splits (the enrichment weights span roughly 0.05–3 in P1 (ε = 2) and are more extreme in the switch blocks (ε ≈ 2.23 at ω = 0, 5.93 at P3's ω = 0.4), far from uniform sampling; for random splits the crossed test R4 shows a bias toward the split (§3.14), but the mask spectrum is only associated with the learned frequencies (EXP-015, seed-coupled; EXP-014 pilot)); time scales are counted in optimizer steps at lr 10⁻³; the lock-in does not follow the step count alone and summed learning rate was not confirmed as its clock (R3, §3.10), and the reversal of §3.4 held at double learning rate (switch at 12 vs 50 steps; at double learning rate 25 steps already hold, §3.10) but not on its switching side at 0.3× weight decay, where the switched networks mostly did not generalize (R2, §3.13); the 25/50-step window is therefore measured only at lr 10⁻³, wd 1; functional measures do not separate identity from energy;
resets localize sensitivity, not mechanism; in the same model class (one-layer transformer, d = 128, full-batch AdamW lr 1e-3, wd 1; p = 59) Khanh (2026, §7, App. A) reports suggestive
evidence (three pairs per task) that transplanting the weight direction of attention, MLP or unembedding alone each pulls the frequency identity towards the donor, so our weak MLP *reset*
effect at a 50-step switch must not be read as "the MLP carries no frequency identity"; many design decisions were informed by pilots on known seeds (each registration lists them). Final models are measured 2000–4000 steps after the switch; whether the persistence survives much longer training is untested beyond that (post-grokking loss of generalization has been reported for add 113, with Muon and in 4/5 runs of an AdamW reference; Janati et al. 2026, arXiv 2608.07436). *Post hoc (session 34, N29 seeds already used for K4c, not a confirmation):* between 2000 and 4000 steps after a switch at 50 (all runs generalized long before), the log power ratio of old over new targets does not decay but grows slightly (half-difference D of the two switch directions +3.68 → +4.12, mean change +0.44 [0.26, 0.62], |D| smaller in 4/20 seeds); after a switch at 25 the new targets keep gaining (D −1.60 → −2.26, 20/20). Within this horizon the winner of the early race is sharpened, not eroded (`lab/confirm/N29_posthoc_decay_s34.py`), as expected if the post-grokking clean-up phase under weight decay (Nanda et al. 2023) removes whichever set the circuit does not use; this is an interpretation, not a test. All persistence results use AdamW with β = (0.9, 0.98); part of the persistence is carried by the first moment (K4b), so its size is expected to depend on the optimizer and β₁ (untested; a β₁/β₂ pilot is drafted, EXP-081).
