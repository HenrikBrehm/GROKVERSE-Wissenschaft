  F_new comes from the final top-5 frequencies of the switched runs after subtracting A, yet the binomial calibration treats it as an independent random subset of C. C is itself conditioned on final pure-run top-5 sets, and the early-growth score uses the same datasets and initialization; for switches at 25 and 50, pure-F at step 20 is even the identical trajectory prefix of the outcome-generating run. The Monte Carlo self-test samples uniform indices and reproduces none of this selection. Ordinary early-to-final persistence can therefore produce a significant “gestuetzt” result without the claimed shared-support mechanism. Test 2 only compares two rankings on the same selected outcomes and cannot repair Test 1’s absolute p-value.
  Recommendation: Calibrate against a null that reruns the complete A/final-top-5/F_new selection pipeline, or evaluate a prespecified score on genuinely held-out runs or pairs. Do not emit the Test 1 verdict from Bin(m, 0.25) until that selection-aware null is established.
- [high] The primary score does not establish positive pre-lock-in support (lab/experiments/EXP-036-init-conflict/analyze_p36b.py:35-53)
  shared_q ranks relative growth but never requires growth to be positive. If every candidate shrinks in both pure runs, the least-shrinking candidate still ranks first and can count as a success, contradicting the question of whether both datasets “push” it. Separately, TAU=20 is assumed to precede lock-in only from old initializations; no code measures or gates on the lock-in time for each new initialization. A pair that locks by step 20 would turn post-selection amplification into purported pre-lock-in evidence. The same issue makes Test 2 an unequal-information predictive comparison, not evidence against an initialization mechanism.
  Recommendation: Require positive, materially sized growth in both arms in addition to rank, and make a pair evaluable only when its own switch runs demonstrate that tau precedes lock-in. Otherwise use an earlier prespecified tau such as 5 or 10 and narrow the interpretation to prediction rather than mechanism.
- [medium] Unchanged run IDs can silently skip the requested spectra (lab/experiments/EXP-001-freq-ban/freq_ban.py:237-243)
  The spectra schedule is excluded from run_id, but completed-run reuse happens immediately afterward without checking result.spectra_at or the stored steps. Running an existing configuration with --spectra-at can therefore exit successfully while producing none of the requested observations. In a resumed EXP-036 queue containing pre-amendment outputs, training would be skipped and the missing spectra discovered only during analysis, after the queue has spent substantial time on the remaining runs.
  Recommendation: Before accepting a completed run, verify that its actual spectra_steps contain every requested step. If not, fail immediately with a force-rerun instruction or include the logging schedule in the artifact identity.
