# Which training pairs a network sees steers which Fourier features it learns — and the choice starts to persist within ~25 steps

*One-page summary for enquiries (supervision, novelty check, compute). Draft, session 41, updated session 59 (2026-09-30: item 5, limits, next question; previous version in git 2ba48590); numbers from [[FINDINGS]] and [[paper/draft_short]], checked by an independent agent. Not for publication as is.*

**Core finding.** In small networks that grok modular arithmetic, the *composition of the training set* (which input pairs are shown, at fixed one-hot tokens) steers which of the equally valid Fourier features the embedding learns; the choice starts to persist after ~25 full-batch steps, and its timing is consistent with a summed-learning-rate clock once Adam's moment windows are rescaled to the same unit.

**Strongest confirmed results.**
1. **Steering.** Engineered splits enrich five prescribed frequencies in the embedding at step 2000: add 113 Δ_pow = 8.24 [7.92, 8.54], mul 113 8.09, add 97 6.11, dihedral group D₅₉ 2.64 (irreps, before grokking), 50/50 seeds each. It holds at ½× and 2× learning rate, 0.3× and 3× weight decay, train fractions 0.2 and 0.5 (6 cells × 15 seeds, 15/15 each), in a shared-embedding MLP (8.89, step 10 000), a 4×-wide transformer (7.84), for non-modular integer addition (2.35; representation only, no generalization) (15 seeds each), and in a two-layer transformer (6.15, 10/10).
2. **Faster grokking** (add 113). Engineered splits generalize ×6.5 [5.3, 7.9] faster than a uniform random split and ×7.5 [6.5, 8.6] faster than a control with the same number of pairs per output (geometric means of times capped at 8000 steps; 20/20 units each). In a pilot dose curve over concentration (prediction committed before the runs; not a confirmation) the time saturates as predicted; the low-concentration end is steeper than predicted (Fig. 7).
3. **Early lock-in and switch cost** (add 113). The sign of an ablation contrast (old vs new target frequencies removed from the embedding) reverses between a data switch at step 25 and at step 50 (0.60 and 0.90 accuracy, 20 seeds; embedding energy points the same way, so reliance is not separated from energy). A switch at step 25 delays grokking ≈ 1.8× (29/30 seeds), whereas a switch to a pair-disjoint split with the *same* targets cost no time on average (−0.15 log2, 8/25 slower; difference to a switch with other targets +0.81 log2 [0.57, 1.04], 24/25; a phase conflict is not excluded).
4. **Clock of the lock-in** (add 113). At equal summed learning rate, the dependence on step count is largely explained by Adam's moment windows (both betas), which are fixed in steps: rescaling them to equal length in summed learning rate shifts persistence as predicted (+0.161 [0.132, 0.192], 36/36 seeds at ¼× learning rate; +0.057, 34/36 at 2×) and reduces the difference between 100 steps at ¼× and 25 default steps to about a tenth (+0.015, equivalent within ±0.1).
5. **Beyond full batch and beyond the toy model.** The steering holds under i.i.d. minibatch AdamW (add 113; Δ_pow 9.43 [9.06, 9.81] at batch 256 and 9.60 [8.79, 10.46] at batch 64, 10/10 fresh seeds each). In a 13.9 M-parameter transformer trained from scratch on TinyStories text with interleaved arithmetic facts (about 79 % of the tokens, tied embeddings), engineered fact splits steered the number-token embedding (Δ_pow 5.32 [4.86, 5.73], 10/10), and removing the run's own five target frequencies lowered median held-out accuracy from 0.98 to 0.05, against 0.98 for the other split's targets (contrast positive in 10/10 seeds). One configuration; the number counts carry the targets as well.

**Method.** Every result above except the dose curve comes from a registration written before its block ran, a frozen analysis manifest (commit ID, code hashes), reserved fresh seeds never used in exploration, Holm correction within each family, and minimum effects or equivalence margins fixed in advance. About 95 completed exploratory experiments (123 planned or run), including negative and unclear ones, are kept and reported separately.

**What is known, what we did not find.** Fourier circuits with seed-dependent key frequencies (Nanda et al. 2023) and algorithm choice by hyperparameters (Zhong et al. 2023); competing frequencies decided by an initial advantage (He et al. 2026a); sampling that determines which frequencies are learned first (Zhou, Zhang & Xu 2024); data *order* as a channel that sets the learned frequency (LeDoux 2026); Fourier statistics of the input encoding setting the order of irreps (Marchetti et al. 2026); critical learning periods (Achille et al. 2019). For result 4, summed learning rate as the natural time axis (Tissue et al. 2024) and scaling 1 − β with the step size (Barakat & Bianchi 2021) are known, and Adam's step-clock moments were shown to matter for data-order noise in fine-tuning (Sweeney 2026); result 4 applies these principles to a new quantity. For result 3, the dependence of a critical-period cost on task relatedness is known qualitatively (Kleinman et al. 2024). *Not found in our searches (Sept. 2026; preliminary, needs expert review):* steering by pair composition at fixed one-hot tokens across tasks and architectures, the 25–50-step lock-in window with its switch cost, and its moment-window clock.

**Limits.** Toy tasks (modular addition and multiplication, integer addition, D₅₉), small one- and two-layer models and one small language-model configuration (6 layers), AdamW only (full batch unless stated; minibatch tested for the steering); lock-in results (3, 4) only for addition mod 113 in a one-layer transformer and full batch; the functional measure follows embedding energy (no use beyond energy shown).

**Next question (needs compute and supervision).** That data selection shapes *what and how fast* language models learn is well known (data curation, mixing, pruning), and pre-trained LLMs are known to add with Fourier/helix features (Zhou, Fu et al. 2024; Kantamneni & Tegmark 2025). Item 5 shows, in one configuration, that engineered fact splits steer the number-token embedding of a small language model trained from scratch (the number counts carry the targets as well). Open, and not found in our search: whether this still works at a few percent arithmetic data (a confirmation at about 9 % is registered and running), and whether fine-tuning a *pre-trained* model on selected facts adds the prescribed frequencies against its existing number features (a pilot with Pythia-160M is queued).
